GUIDE · 6 MIN

What Are Caffeine Pouches?

Caffeine pouches are a compact format built around one simple idea: delivering a caffeine-containing product without needing a drink. The format is easy to understand, but strength and serving guidance matter.

The format

A pouch sits between traditional beverages and other portable energy formats. From a product-design perspective, its main advantages are portability, consistency of unit size and a clear way to communicate strength on the packaging.

Strength matters more than hype

A higher number is not automatically a better product. A range should help people understand what they are choosing, and high-caffeine variants need especially careful serving instructions and warnings.

What 69 is doing now

The current 69 site is still a concept. The seven flavors, three strength tiers and supporting formula ingredients are being used to test the brand architecture, user experience and visual system before final formulation decisions are made.

Concept note: final product classification, labeling, dosage, safety language and age restrictions should be reviewed for the markets where the product will actually be sold.
FORMULA · 5 MIN

Caffeine + L-Theanine: Why Pair Them?

Caffeine and L-theanine are often discussed together in performance-oriented products because they play very different roles in the formula story.

Caffeine is the obvious signal

Caffeine is the stimulant component. It is the ingredient consumers immediately understand, which makes dosage clarity especially important.

L-Theanine is the supporting concept

In the current 69 prototype, L-theanine is positioned as a complementary ingredient rather than a reason to make aggressive claims. The final amount should be chosen only after the complete formula is defined.

Why restraint helps the brand

A premium product does not need exaggerated language. Clear ingredient disclosure, honest dose communication and a strong experience can do more for trust than overpromising outcomes.

The current L-theanine values on the website are placeholders for design purposes.
STRENGTH · 7 MIN

75 vs 150 vs 250 mg: Designing a Strength Ladder

A three-tier architecture can make a range easy to shop, but the top tier needs to be treated differently from the entry tier.

75 mg — entry concept

This is presented as the lowest level in the prototype and gives the range a clear starting point.

150 mg — core concept

The middle tier is positioned as the center of the range. It should still be accompanied by clear serving guidance once the final product is developed.

250 mg — high-caffeine concept

This is deliberately marked MAX. A high number can create marketing impact, but product design has to prioritize responsible use over visual escalation. Whether this tier should exist at all is a formulation and regulatory decision, not just a branding decision.

The site is a visual prototype, not final consumption guidance. Do not use the mock strength ladder as personal dosing advice.
